Notes from discussion:

1) How does Columbus try to make the islands seem very important to Isabella + Ferdinand?

• Profit: The Native people said that there was much gold and spices

• Fertile: the land was very fertile, making it good for building towns and villages

• Naming: Columbus named the islands after the king and queen in honor of them.

2) Physical descriptions of the islands – motives for his voyage?

• The island was very tropical

• There were many new species of plants and animals

• Lands were very fertile and good for growing crops

• There were villages and towns the Native people lived in.

• A motive for Columbus's voyage was gold, land, and slaves

3) Why do you think C might've been "prepared" or "made ready" to see what he wanted to see?

• At first, so many indicators that the land he landed at was not east Asia, meaning he convinced himself that he landed there

• he also probably wanted his trip to be a success to impress the king and queen in order to be funded in the future for his expeditions

• Columbus came to the islands of the Native Americans and told them that the people from Heaven were coming and he gave them "stuff" just so that they would give him stuff in return and treat him like a god

4-5) Self-promotional account proving he reached the islands, or factual + objective report on what he saw?

• We question almost everything Columbus claims bc he really is unreliable and biased

• People, trade, and the mention of gold was likely true

• The entire letter is basically self promotion

• Insults the Native Americans, then claims that he protected them from getting cheated (acting like he is a really good and kind Christian)

• Asks for a "small" favor from king + queen at the end of letter

6) Given what C says and does to the Native Americans, what do you think his plans are for them?

7) Culture of Tainos as seen through C's eyes?

• He looked down upon them because he viewed them as unsophisticated and like animals, he also thought that their land belonged to Genghis Khan!!

Wednesday, 8/26/26

Summary Of Class
  • Opened with ~15 minutes to get going on the R+A'ing, and I came around to lightly check the work from last night.
  • Then a lecture from me on the historical context for exploration in 15th-century Europe. We talked about what a "feudal" society actually looked like. Lords owned the land and serfs farmed it. Lords get services (farming, taxes, soldiers) and serfs got protection and a place to live. The "middle class" of merchants and craftspeople was a very small slice of the population. The Roman Catholic Church had an enormous role in daily life, and Church and State were thoroughly intertwined politically and in everyday life!
  • We sorted all of that into categories of religion, politics, society, culture, and economics. How is all of that different from our modern world today?
  • We also put "capitalism" up as a term to define. Individual property ownership of "stuff," and private enterprise. The 'means of production' owned privately! Worth touching on now because the modern, interconnected, globalized, capitalistic economy we live in today starts to come into being as a result of 15th-century exploration.
  • We ran out of time for the Crash Course video, so we'll watch it tomorrow (or soon) instead.
